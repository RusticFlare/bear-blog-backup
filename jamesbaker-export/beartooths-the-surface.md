This is my entry to this month’s [Bear Blog Carnival](tab:https://grizzlygazette.bearblog.dev/carnival/) – [An album that got you through a hard time](tab:https://ev-fae.quest/bear-blog-carnival-an-album-that-got-you-through-a-hard-time/).

---

I’ve been lucky in my life to not have been through a _truly_ hard time. There isn’t an album that’s saved my life or anything like that. But, as it says on my homepage at the moment, <q>Heavy metal and running are my therapists</q>. The more stressed I am, the heavier the music I’ll listen to. At the moment, that’s going to be something like **CABAL**, **Thus Spoke Zarathustra** or **Lorna Shore**.

But this blog is about <cite>The Surface</cite> by **Beartooth**, because it was the album that got me through a series of job interviews.

![The album cover of The Surface by Beartooth](https://bear-images.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/jamesbaker/ab67616d0000b27318e8a8ba2fc255d33d29c25b-1.webp)

Once I’m in a job interview, I’m usually alright. I enjoy talking shop, and time tends to fly. It’s the buildup that stresses me out. And last time I was interviewing, <cite>The Surface</cite> was the perfect album to put on before an interview, to turn that stress into feeling pumped and ready to go.

<cite>The Surface</cite> is about being in a better place after battling mental health issues, with songs titled <cite>Sunshine!</cite>, <cite>The Better Me</cite> and <cite>Might Love Myself</cite>. But the song that _really_ energised me was <cite>Doubt Me</cite> – an absolute anthem. Here’s the chorus’s lyrics:

> Remember every time you doubt me\
> It makes me stronger than before\
> When you doubt me\
> It fuels the fire even more\
> So give me all you got\
> I can’t wait to watch you rot\
> If there's one thing you should learn about me\
> Don’t ever fucking doubt me

I’ve been listening to Beartooth since their first EP <cite>Sick</cite>, and right through their first four albums <cite>Disgusting</cite>, <cite>Aggressive</cite>, <cite>Disease</cite> and <cite>Below</cite>. Those releases were heavier, and the titles reflect that the artist was consistently in a dark place. I think the shift <cite>The Surface</cite> makes to a more upbeat sound is a big part of why it’s so good at channeling my negative emotions into positive ones – I believed the singer had gone through just that.

I also like that the album cover’s pink.

So there you go. Give it a listen – maybe when you’re working out or running. Or just bang your head and sing along.

--- 

Beartooth’s next album, <cite>Pure Ecstasy</cite>, comes out next month. Hopefully it’s just as good.
