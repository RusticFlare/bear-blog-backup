As a kid, I used to get up on Sunday mornings and watch the repeat of <cite>Match of the Day</cite> for the Premier League highlights. I didn’t have Sky for the live games, and I was too young to watch the news. I can remember a grandparent following the scores on Teletext, but I never learnt how to use that.

I really got into the habit of watching the live Saturday night showings when I got my first job working Saturday afternoons pushing trollies at Asda. I’d finish when the store shut at 10pm and get home just in time for the first match.

Today, most football fans I know, follow the scores live on their phones, either via push notifications, or constantly checking a live page. I, however, stay deliberately in the dark – looking forward to finding out the scores through the ten-to-fifteen minutes given to each fixture. It maintains that unique unpredictability of watch live sports… mostly.

They always play the most exciting matches first. You know that when they show a yellow card, a second yellow is likely to follow. When you see a substitution, the player coming on will probably have a big impact on the game.

Despite all of that I still find it the best way of finding out the scores. I gasp at the misses, and celebrate the goals.

And if I really want to follow a game live, I can watch at the pub, or listen on the radio.

All my friends and family now know not to spoil the results for me. And when I’m sat with other fans on a Saturday afternoon I ask them not to tell me the scores.

Then, there’s one really frustrating thing that can catch you out when you watch <cite>Match of the Day</cite> live: the news. Right before it starts, the news finishes with the sports segment! So you have to be really carful when turning to BBC One that you don’t get the results ruined.
