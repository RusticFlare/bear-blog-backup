I want to share one of my favourite artists: Martin Parr. An English photographer who sadly passed away at the end of last year.

I first came across Martin Parr’s work when I was getting into street photography and started reading books on it. This is the first photo of his I remember seeing (from his series <cite>[Benidorm](tab:https://www.magnumphotos.com/arts-culture/society-arts-culture/martin-parr-benidorm/)</cite>, a Spanish city popular with British tourists):

Martin Parr is known for photographing the details – he got close. He used vibrant and bold colours. He captured the British enjoying their leisure time, from the seaside to the cricket pitch. And he did it with a beautiful mix of humour and respect. You never feel like you’re laughing at the subject, but he makes you appreciate that the world is a funny and weird place.

I own the book <cite>[ToiletMartin PaperParr](tab:https://shoptoiletpaper.com/products/toiletmartin-paperparr)</cite>, which is a collaboration between him and the irreverent art studio Toiletpaper. It pairs each *impossible* image from Toiletpaper with a *real* image from Martin Parr.

Last year when we were in Kyoto we were lucky enough to see an exhibition of Martin Parr’s photos of global tourism – mostly from his <cite>[Small World](tab:https://martinparrfoundation.org/product/small-world-new-revised-edition-signed/)</cite> series. (Yes, we know we were some of those global tourists!) It also included some photos Martin Parr had taken in Kyoto especially for the exhibition.

Martin Parr inspires me to make my own street photography more fun. Not just the images, but also the process. I want to see the world through his lens, in all its vivid and absurd detail.

Check out [his website](tab:https://martinparr.com/) (which includes [his blog](tab:https://martinparr.com/blog/)). I love how the photo of him on [his introduction](tab:https://martinparr.com/introduction/) changes every time you refresh the page.
