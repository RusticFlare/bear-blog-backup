We just got back from an eleven night holiday to Italy. We bounced around a bit, staying in Bologna, Riva del Garda, Verona and Venice.

My favourite thing to do in each place? Walk around with my camera. For this trip I decided to take one camera (Leica MP) and one lens (35mm F1.5), and shoot one type of film (Portra 400) – apart from one roll of Ultramax.

Having a camera at the ready puts me in a more observant state of mind, I’m looking around for anything interesting to capture.

I can easily spend hours walking around a new city doing street photography. When combined with the research Jess does about where things are, we can easily stop at interesting bars/restaurants/cafés/shops. I love it because it means I don’t need a busy itinerary while I’m away – often exploring is enough.

Taking pictures as a tourist made me think of the photographer [Martin Parr](tab:https://martinparr.com/). Last year we saw a gallery in Kyoto (Japan) of his work photographing global tourism and we recently watched [I Am Martin Parr](tab:https://releasing.dogwoof.com/i-am-martin-parr). Trying to spot what he’d have taken photos of made me appreciate that tourism is a massive part of each place we visited. From the “TOURISTS GO HOME” graffiti in Venice, to the shops in Verona. I live in a historic city (York, UK) and I know that without the tourists we wouldn’t still have a thriving town centre with shops, pubs, restaurants and street food.

Anyway, I’ve posted my film off to the lab to get it developed. I can’t wait to get the scans back. The anticipation of seeing your own holiday photos – not something many people go through any more. It’s genuinely exciting.
